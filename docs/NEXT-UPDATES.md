# One More Life — major expansion proposal

This is the historical catalogue of the approved 180-item scope. The active
sequence is the v0.90–v0.99 checkpoint map in [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md), with
the current campaign details in [FOUR-UPDATE-CAMPAIGN.md](FOUR-UPDATE-CAMPAIGN.md).
The owner approved substantial local review packages at each ready checkpoint;
v1.0 remains the final consolidated release. The old v0.28–v0.34 table below is
retained for traceability and does not set the future release cadence.

Use the [completion register](COMPLETION-REGISTER.md) for the accepted scope and

## Historical preview record

The earlier v0.28, v0.29, and v0.30 entries describe development previews from
the original proposal. Their coverage documents remain available in `releases/`;
they are not the active schedule or a claim that the full proposal was completed.

## Direction

Make the simulator richer through people with continuing lives, practical
constraints, meaningful choices and lasting consequences. Keep room for humour,
ambition, quiet years, adult drama and unusual life paths. Realism means coherent
relationships and trade-offs, not making the player perform every household task.
Keep the dark palettes, charming varied panels, easy navigation and optional
minigames. No universal playstyle or life outcome should count as the correct one.

Existing foundations include healthcare, body ageing, housing, transport,
workplace progression, NPC relationships, local stories, criminal justice,
investments and special careers. The list below mixes new systems with deliberate
expansion of those foundations; it does not pretend they are all currently absent.

UI, balance, save migration, minigame repairs and regression checks belong in
every update; they are not postponed until v0.34. Dependencies may change the
order. The first batch starts with family records and continuity, not all domains
at once. These version names are working titles.

Campaign planning targets: 600–1,000 distinct new scenes, around 80 connected
multi-year arcs, 20–30 additional careers or routes, 8–12 new minigames, and 4–6
original story campaigns. These are sizing targets across updates, not promises
for the next release. Count branch scenes once, separate revised existing scenes
from new ones, and reduce the totals if they encourage repetitive filler.

## Full scope: 180 additions and refinements

### 1. Character continuity and generations

1. Give important NPCs persistent education, qualifications, employment and salary records.
2. Record personal assets, debts, medical history, criminal history and household membership.
3. Show a family tree with marriages, separations, adoption, stepfamilies and deceased relatives.
4. Preview the child's actual circumstances and unresolved commitments before switching.
5. Let former player characters continue their recorded obligations and ambitions as NPCs.
6. Preserve personal memories and ongoing story threads when changing the active character.
7. Distinguish inheritance, gifts, shared property and a change of viewpoint in the accounts.
8. Add family-business handovers, contested estates and meaningful heirloom histories.
9. Allow descendants to react to family reputations without mechanically inheriting moral guilt.

### 2. People with motives and relationships

10. Expand closeness into visible trust, respect, affection, conflict and reliability where useful.
11. Give people goals, preferences, boundaries and practical constraints that influence their choices.
12. Make promises, favors, debts and broken commitments persist in a readable memory list.
13. Add relationship drift, long-distance friendship, reconnection and changing life priorities.
14. Deepen NPC-to-NPC links so friendships, rivalries and romances affect the wider cast.
15. Let people initiate invitations, requests, support, confrontation and reconciliation.
16. Add repair paths that require time and consistent behavior, rather than one perfect gift.
17. Show multiple interpretations of a disagreement without inventing a universal best answer.
18. Summarize each important person's recent life so their agency is visible to the player.

### 3. Everyday time, energy and routines

19. Connect work, study, commuting, caregiving and leisure through a understandable time budget.
20. Add optional seasonal checkpoints inside a year without requiring constant calendar clicks.
21. Let routines carry on automatically, with clear annual costs and benefits.
22. Provide routine presets for student life, shift work, parenting, recovery and retirement.
23. Add sustainable habits, fatigue, rest and competing priorities with gradual effects.
24. Offer delegation, family help and paid services when responsibilities become overwhelming.
25. Add cooking, errands, paperwork, household repairs and lost-item scenes as meaningful choices.
26. Let celebrations and quiet satisfaction coexist with setbacks; avoid a compulsory crisis every year.
27. Explain why an action is unavailable and provide a useful alternative where possible.

### 4. Homes, neighborhoods and practical life

29. Expand roommates with shared bills, boundaries, visitors, chores and departure agreements.

### 5. Childhood and teenage life

### 6. Education and lifelong learning

## The first release: concrete scope

v0.28 should build the first usable form of richer NPC records and family history,
with backward-compatible migration. Prioritize transfer correctness, parent/child
links, education/jobs/money, a family-tree interface, visible recent NPC events,
and context-sensitive family/relationship scenes. Keep richer dormant records for
important characters while limiting expensive yearly simulation of distant NPCs.

The first 40–60 scenes should cover invitations, changing ambitions, shared
expenses, first independence, broken promises, work/family conflicts, moving,
caregiving and reconciliation. About 10–15 should become small connected arcs.
Choices should create distinct remembered states; follow-ups must account for
the actual earlier choice and the people still involved.

Build annual-summary and navigation improvements alongside that work. Fix any
existing critical issue before adding more content to the affected system. Make
later-life continuity richer without forcing every NPC to use the full active
player simulation, which would create performance and maintenance problems.

## When an addition is ready

- It is reachable under appropriate circumstances, including age, place, era and mode.
- It offers meaningful choices or useful information; extra outcomes are not filler.
- Its costs, uncertainty, ownership and consequences are understandable.
- It records the decision and survives saving, loading and changing generations.
- It reacts to who was involved and what the player actually chose.
- It respects theme preferences and the game's dark, readable presentation.
- It does not create an unlimited loop or make a single playstyle dominant.
- Required content, UI, migration and minigame checks pass, with representative screenshots inspected.
- Its source and asset notices are recorded; public documentation matches the delivered package.

## Intended identity

The best reason to choose this game should be: “The people remember what I did,
the world changes while I am busy, and I can live very different lives.” Broad
feature coverage supports that identity, but the current preview does not claim
parity with or superiority to another life simulator.
